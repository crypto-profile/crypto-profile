# 0010: HKDF under a profile

Date: 2026-11-01. Status: accepted.

## Decision

**The function.** `hkdf::derive(&admitted, algorithm, secret, salt, info, len)` is HKDF as RFC 5869 §2.2-§2.3 defines it, the two-step key derivation that SP 800-56C Rev. 2 §5 approves; RFC 5869 "specifies a version of" it (SP 800-56C Rev. 2, p. 20). Extraction and expansion happen in one call, in RFC 5869's byte order, `T(i) = HMAC(PRK, T(i-1) | info | i)`; it is not a generic SP 800-108 feedback-mode KDF, which BSI TR-02102-1 §B.1.1 (p. 73) distinguishes for interoperability. HKDF-SHA256, HKDF-SHA384 and HKDF-SHA512 are offered. SHA-1, SHA-224, SHA-512/t and SHA3 are not, as for HMAC (decision 0008). *(Amended 2026-11-01, #33: the function is `kdf::derive(&admitted, secret, &kdf::Spec::Hkdf { algorithm, salt, info }, len)` and returns `kdf::DerivedKey`; the unreleased `hkdf` module became `kdf`, [decision 0012](0012-api-coherence-and-non-exhaustive.md).)*

**No public extract.** SP 800-56C Rev. 2 §5 says the key-derivation key "shall not be an output" and is destroyed after use (pp. 17, 20); BSI TR-02102-1 §B.1.1 (p. 73) says the MAC output must not be shortened. `derive` is the only entry point. The pseudorandom key exists only inside the module's one-shot `HKDF()`. Expand alone, extract alone, TLS 1.3 `HKDF-Expand-Label`, SP 800-108 KDFs and KMAC are not offered.

**The salt.** `salt: Option<&[u8]>`. `None` and `Some(&[])` are both replaced by HashLen zero bytes before the module is called. That is RFC 5869's default, and, after the zero padding of FIPS 198-1, the same HMAC key as SP 800-56C Rev. 2's default of one hash block of zeros (§5.1, p. 18), so the derived bytes are those of either default. AWS-LC does not mark an HKDF call with a non-NULL, empty salt approved (`HKDF_verify_service_indicator`), and aws-lc-rs passes an empty salt that way, so the explicit salt also keeps every call on the module's approved path.

**`info`.** `info` is required. SP 800-56A Rev. 3 §5.8.2 (p. 54) says the fixed information *should* bind the derived key to the transaction (identifiers, contributed public keys, nonces), so an empty `info` is not against the standards; but AWS-LC does not mark an HKDF call with an empty `info` approved. Every profile except `unrestricted` refuses an empty `info` with `CryptoError::KdfInfoEmpty` (`kdf_info_may_be_empty`), so those profiles reach only the approved service. `unrestricted` is the inventory, makes no approval claim, and derives with it (maintainer, 2026-11-01).

**Lengths.** The output is 1 to 255 × HashLen bytes (8160, 12240 and 16320): RFC 5869 §2.3 caps L at 255 × HashLen, and SP 800-56C Rev. 2 §5.1 makes L a positive integer, so 0 is refused, although the module accepts it. A longer output is refused before the module is called, with `CryptoError::KdfOutputLength`. The secret may be of any length but empty: RFC 5869 and SP 800-56C set no length for the input keying material, and deriving from nothing is a misuse no source describes, refused with `CryptoError::KdfSecretEmpty`. That refusal is the one input rule the specifications do not state; the maintainer can strike it. Refusals come in a fixed order: admission (`NotInProfile`, or `Unavailable` for a KDF the module line lacks), then `info`, then the output length, then the secret. *(Amended 2026-11-01, #33: before admission, a `kdf::Spec::Hkdf` whose algorithm is not an HKDF, a one-step KDF, is refused with `CryptoError::KdfSpecMismatch`.)*

**Profile cells.** Each cell follows that profile's own governing texts and what they cite (decision 0008):

| Profile | KDFs, in preference order | Chain |
|---|---|---|
| `unrestricted` | HKDF-SHA512, HKDF-SHA384, HKDF-SHA256 | The inventory |
| `preferred` | HKDF-SHA512, HKDF-SHA384, HKDF-SHA256 | The ISM names no KDF among its general controls. It cites SP 800-56C Rev. 2 (p. 13), which names the two-step HMAC method, and admits SHA-2 (ISM-1766, p. 11). The ISM cites SP 800-56C Rev. 2 for combining the components of a hybrid key encapsulation; reading that citation as reaching the construction generally was accepted by the maintainer (2026-11-01). HKDF-SHA256 carries the ISM's 2030 statement on SHA-256 (p. 11) and is handled as HMAC-SHA256 is: kept until a later transition point, with a warning before removal. |
| `cnsa2` | HKDF-SHA384, HKDF-SHA512 | CNSSP 15 (December 2024) names no KDF. ¶8a (p. 5) names RFC 9151, which approves SHA-384 for HKDF in TLS 1.3 (§4); Annex B (p. B-1) admits SHA-384 and SHA-512. SHA-384 first, following RFC 9151 and the MAC order. |
| `cnsa1` | HKDF-SHA384; HKDF-SHA256 with `Options::cnsa1_unclassified_nss` | CNSSP 15 (20 October 2016) Annex B (p. B-1) names SHA-384 alone; RFC 9151 §4 names HKDF with SHA-384. ¶2b's near-term SHA-256 allowance (p. B-2) is not limited to MACs. |

**The near-term option widens.** `Options::cnsa1_unclassified_nss` now covers message authentication and key derivation: it admits HKDF-SHA256 under `cnsa1` on the terms of CNSSP 15 (2016) Annex B ¶2b, as it admits HMAC-SHA256 (maintainer, 2026-11-01). Decision 0008 is amended in place. *(Amended 2026-11-03, #45: it covers hashing too, admitting SHA-256 hashing under `cnsa1` by this decision's reading of ¶2b (maintainer); [decision 0014](0014-hashing.md).)*

**Module cells.** On the 3.x line the three cells are `Approved`: Table 5 of each certificate's security policy (#5314 and #5298, p. 10) lists "KDA HKDF Sp800-56Cr1" with HMAC SHA2-256, SHA2-384 and SHA2-512, reference SP 800-56C Rev. 2, and Table 12 lists "Key Derivation with KDA HKDF" as an approved service with indicator 1 (p. 25 static, p. 26 dynamic), with no TLS qualifier. On the 4.x dynamic build they are `AlgorithmTested` on A7855; the same capability, with the same parameters, appears on A7856-A7859 and A7863 (x86) and on A7871 and A7872 (arm64). On the 4.x static build they are `Unverified`. *(Amended 2026-11-03, #44: `AlgorithmTested` on A7902, the static build's counterpart of A7855; [decision 0004](0004-algorithm-tested.md) §4.)* No certificate or policy lists "KDA TwoStep", a Cr2-labelled HKDF or a TLS 1.3 KDF, so `Protocol(Tls13)` stays as decision 0004 §3 leaves it.

**Approval of an admitted set.** `assert_admitted_approved` checks FIPS mode first, then refuses with `CryptoError::KdfInfoMayBeEmpty` an `Admitted` whose profile admits KDFs and an empty `info`, then walks the cells, MACs first, then KDFs (maintainer ruling, 2026-11-01). Today that refuses `unrestricted`, on both module lines: `unrestricted` admits an empty `info`, which the module does not approve. It promises no approval; it also admits entries no module approves (X25519, ChaCha20-Poly1305), so `assert_module_approves` could never pass it either. The refusal does not depend on the other families: an `Admitted` with no KDFs is not refused for it. `derive` under `unrestricted` still derives with an empty `info`; the check reports approval, it does not change what `derive` does. On the 3.x line every other profile passes, because every MAC and KDF cell is `Approved`. *(Amended 2026-11-01, #33: the assertion takes the families the caller uses, and the `KdfInfoMayBeEmpty` refusal applies only when `Family::Kdf` is named, so `unrestricted` with `[Family::Mac]` walks the MAC cells. Maintainer ruling; [decision 0012](0012-api-coherence-and-non-exhaustive.md).)*

**Tested-range residuals.** The certificates print "Shared Secret Length: 224-2048 Increment 8" and "Derived Key Length: 2048" (bits), with the fixed-info pattern "uPartyInfo||vPartyInfo||l". The crate follows the specifications, not those ranges: a secret shorter than 28 or longer than 256 bytes, an output other than 256 bytes, and an `info` of any other shape run on paths the certificates did not sample. Each 4.x cell's `cavp` text names the tested ranges and the fixed-info pattern (decision 0004 §5).

**Inside the crate.** The backend body keeps a crate-internal core that applies the RFC rules (output length, empty secret) and the salt replacement without the profile rules, so key agreement (#33) can derive from a shared secret held inside the crate and CatKDF (#34) can call it with `salt = label`, `info = context` (ETSI TS 103 744 V1.2.1 §7.4.2). Nothing public is added beyond `derive`. *(Amended 2026-11-01, #33: the core also refuses an algorithm that is not an HKDF, first.)*

## Consequences

- A format that derives with an empty `info` cannot be produced under any profile but `unrestricted`; the crate refuses it rather than reaching the module's non-approved path.
- `sealbox` passes its `domain` as the HKDF salt, so `seal` and `open` refuse an empty `domain` with `CryptoError::SealDomainEmpty` rather than reach the module's non-approved path. No profile governs `sealbox` (`docs/profiles.md` §5). *(Amended 2026-11-02, #33: `sealbox` derives through `kdf` and refuses unless the profile admits ECDH on P-384 and HKDF-SHA384; [decision 0011](0011-ecdh-and-one-step-kdf.md).)* *(Amended 2026-11-03, #44: and AES-256-GCM; [decision 0013](0013-aead.md).)*
- On the 3.x line the module does not meet SP 800-56C Rev. 2 §5's requirement that the key-derivation key "shall be destroyed (along with all other sensitive, locally stored data) after its use" (p. 20): its `HKDF()` returns with the pseudorandom key still in a stack buffer it never cleanses (`aws-lc-fips-sys-0.13.17/aws-lc/crypto/fipsmodule/hkdf/hkdf.c:32-56`). The 4.x module does meet it: its `HKDF()` calls `OPENSSL_cleanse` on that buffer on every exit (`aws-lc-fips-sys-0.14.2/aws-lc/crypto/fipsmodule/hkdf/hkdf.c:50`). The crate cannot fix the 3.x behaviour without changing the validated module. It is a named secret-handling residual (`docs/usage.md` §11).
- The salt may be secret under SP 800-56C Rev. 2 §5.1 ("secret or non-secret", p. 18) and RFC 5869 §3.1, but aws-lc-rs copies it, and `info`, into buffers it does not wipe. The crate supports only a non-secret salt and `info`; a caller must not pass a secret in either. It is a named secret-handling residual (`docs/usage.md` §11).
- No test can observe the module receiving the non-empty salt, because aws-lc-rs keeps its approved-service indicator crate-private. The substitution (`salt_for`) is pinned by its own test and by the mutation gate.
- Later families (ECDH, ML-KEM, RSA-OAEP) join `Admitted` under the same rule. *(Note 2026-11-02: ECDH has joined, with the one-step KDF beside HKDF in `kdf`; [decision 0011](0011-ecdh-and-one-step-kdf.md).)*
